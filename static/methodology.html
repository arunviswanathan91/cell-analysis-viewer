Step 01

Patient Cohort and Study Design

Step 01 — Cohort

CPTAC-3 PDAC Dataset

Data Source

What it is: The Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) is a National Cancer Institute program that performs deep molecular profiling of cancer tissues. The CPTAC-3 pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma dataset contains bulk RNA-seq data from 140 primary tumor samples along with comprehensive clinical data including BMI.

Data accessed via: The Genomic Data Commons (GDC) using the TCGAbiolinks R package. Raw STAR count matrices were downloaded for all 140 patients with available BMI information.

💡 Why CPTAC-3 Specifically?

CPTAC-3 is one of the largest comprehensively characterized PDAC cohorts with matched BMI data. Other PDAC datasets exist (TCGA-PAAD) but have smaller sample sizes and less complete clinical annotation for metabolic parameters. CPTAC also provides matched proteomic data (not used here but available for future validation).

⚙️ BMI Classification (WHO Standards)
GroupBMI RangenRole
Normal Weight (N)18.5–24.9 kg/m²51Reference group for all comparisons
Overweight (OW)25.0–29.9 kg/m²58Primary transition state — largest group
Obese (OB)≥30.0 kg/m²18Smallest group — Bayesian regularization critical here
🍽️ Why Three Groups and Not Two?

Most obesity studies compare "obese vs non-obese" — like only comparing a restaurant with excellent reviews to one with terrible reviews and ignoring the many-starred restaurants in between. This study explicitly studies the overweight intermediate, which turns out to be the most scientifically interesting group — the tipping point where most microenvironmental reprogramming begins.

Step 02

Bulk Transcriptomic Analysis

Step 02a — Differential Expression

DESeq2 — Group Comparisons

R Package

What it does: DESeq2 normalizes raw RNA-seq count data and tests whether individual genes are statistically different between two groups using a negative binomial model. Applied to compare N vs OW, N vs OB, and OW vs OB.

💡 Why DESeq2 and Not EdgeR or Limma?

All three are valid. DESeq2 uses shrinkage estimators for dispersion, which is particularly beneficial for smaller sample sizes. With n=18 in the obese group, this shrinkage is important. DESeq2 also provides log2 fold changes as output, which are directly usable for KEGG Pathview visualization.

🔬 The Mass Spectrometry Analogy

DESeq2 is like a mass spectrometer — it measures transcript abundance, accounts for total RNA amounts (normalization), and reports which measurements are significantly different between groups.

Step 02b — Pathway Analysis

GSEA — Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

clusterProfiler

What it does: Instead of asking "which individual genes changed?" GSEA asks "which biological pathways are systematically shifted?" It ranks all genes by their DESeq2 test statistics and tests whether genes belonging to a given pathway tend to cluster at the top or bottom of this ranked list.

Databases used: Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes, KEGG pathways, and Reactome pathways. Significance threshold: FDR-adjusted p-value < 0.05. Gene set sizes: 10–500 genes.

💡 Why GSEA Instead of Simple Pathway ORA?

Over-Representation Analysis (ORA) only looks at genes that passed a significance threshold. GSEA uses the full ranked list — including genes with subtle consistent trends. With BMI being a gradual exposure, the true biological signal is spread across many genes with small effects. GSEA captures this diffuse signal that ORA would miss.

🎯 The Sports Team Analogy

ORA asks: "Are the top 50 players disproportionately from Team A?" GSEA asks: "Do Team A's players tend to cluster at the top of the overall ranking, even if none of them individually tops the list?" GSEA is more sensitive because even a team of moderately high performers can be systematically better than average.

Step 02c — Continuous BMI Correlation

LinkedOmics — BMI as Continuous Variable

Web Platform

What it does: LinkedOmics systematically correlates BMI (as a continuous number from 18 to 50+) with every gene's expression across all 140 samples using Pearson correlation. This identifies genes whose expression tracks linearly with BMI — different from DESeq2 which compares discrete groups.

Dataset: CPTAC-3 pancreatic adenocarcinoma (ID-132290), RSEM upper-quantile-normalized log2 tumor expression data. 28,057 gene features tested. Significance: p-value < 0.05.

💡 Why Add This On Top of DESeq2?

DESeq2 misses gradual dose-response patterns within groups. LinkedOmics treats BMI as what it actually is: a continuous spectrum from 18 to 50+. The key finding from this step was that the Primary Immunodeficiency pathway showed the strongest continuous BMI correlation.

📈 The Blood Pressure Analogy

To assess whether blood pressure affects cardiac risk, one approach divides people into "low BP" and "high BP" groups and compares them — that's DESeq2. Another treats blood pressure as a continuous number and correlates it directly — that's LinkedOmics. The continuous approach captures graded relationships that category cutoffs obscure.

Step 03

Single-Cell RNA-seq Reference Construction

Step 03 — scRNA References

Two Custom References Built from GEO

GEO Datasets

Why build custom references? BayesPrism needs a reference that reflects the actual cell types present in PDAC tumors. Generic immune or cancer references built from other cancer types would misidentify cell proportions. Two specialized references were built:

ReferenceSourceCellsPurpose
Whole-TumorGSE242230 (needle biopsies, n=25)35,008 pre-QCAll TME populations: fibroblasts, tumor, acinar, ductal, vascular, neural

💡 Why Two Separate References and Not One?

The CD45+ enriched dataset provides high resolution immune cell states. The whole-tumor dataset captures stromal and tumor populations that would be rare in a CD45+ enriched dataset. Using both separately gives the best of both worlds: fine immune resolution AND stromal coverage.

📚 The Specialist Library Analogy

A specialized immunology reference is best for immune cells (more detail on T cell subtypes), while a separate oncology/pathology reference is best for tumor and stromal cells. Using both together gives more accurate identification than either alone.

💡 Why Adaptive Instead of Fixed Thresholds?

The whole-tumor dataset contains rare stromal and acinar cell types that genuinely have fewer genes than common immune cells. A fixed minimum of "200 genes" might inappropriately exclude these biologically real rare populations. Adaptive thresholds preserve rare populations while still removing genuinely low-quality cells.

📏 The Height Chart Analogy

A fixed threshold of "must be under 165cm" for jockeys applied globally would exclude some perfectly good jockeys from a population where jockeys are naturally taller. An adaptive threshold — "bottom 5% of the specific population" — correctly identifies the smallest individuals regardless of where the overall population sits.

Step 05

Cell Type Annotation

Step 05a — Malignancy

CopyKat — Distinguishing Tumor from Normal Cells

R Package

What it does: CopyKat infers chromosomal copy number variations (CNV) from single-cell RNA expression data. Cancer cells carry large chromosomal gains and losses; normal cells are diploid. By computing CNV profiles, CopyKat classifies each epithelial cell as aneuploid (malignant) or diploid (normal).

⚙️ CopyKat Settings
ngene.chr = 5, win.size = 25, KS.cut = 0.1

Pre-processing: mitochondrial and ribosomal genes removed; genes expressed in <1% of epithelial cells filtered. CopyKat uses diploid reference cells (normal fibroblasts and immune cells) to anchor copy number inference.

💡 Why Not Just Use Marker Genes?

Marker gene approaches can't distinguish malignant from normal ductal epithelial cells — both express EPCAM. CopyKat's CNV-based approach directly identifies genetic abnormalities that define malignancy.

🧬 The Karyotype Analogy

In pathology, karyotyping identifies cancer cells by looking at chromosomal abnormalities. CopyKat does the computational equivalent — it infers these chromosomal abnormalities from RNA expression patterns, identifying cancer cells by their genetic scars rather than just their surface markers.

Step 05b — Tumor Subtyping

Basal-like vs Classical PDAC Subtypes

Module Scoring

Aneuploid (malignant) cells were further divided into basal-like and classical PDAC subtypes using established gene signatures:

  • Basal-like: S100A2, KRT6A, KRT17, SERPINB4, LY6D, HMGA2, TP63, KRT5, DSC3, PKP1
  • Classical: TFF1, LGALS4, TSPAN8, REG4, ST6GALNAC1, CTSE, GATA6, HNF1A, HNF4A, FOXA2, FOXA3, PDX1

Cells with basal score > classical score AND >0.20 → basal-like. Classical score dominant >0.20 → classical. Remainder → unclassified tumor.

Step 05c — Immune Annotation

SingleR Multi-Reference Consensus Strategy

R Package

What it does: SingleR is an automated cell type annotation tool. It compares each cell's expression profile against a reference database of known immune cell types using Spearman correlation. Four references used simultaneously: Monaco Immune, DICE, Blueprint, and Novershtern hematopoietic.

⚙️ SingleR Settings
method = "wilcox", de.n = 50 (main) / 20 (fine) fine.tune = TRUE, tune.thresh = 0.05 (main) / 0.1 (fine) sd.thresh = 1 (main) / 1.5 (fine), prune = TRUE
💡 Why Four References and Not One?

Each reference database has different strengths. Monaco excels at T cell subtypes. DICE is strong for myeloid populations. Blueprint covers hematopoietic lineages broadly. Using four and taking consensus dramatically reduces misclassification compared to any single reference.

🩺 The Second Opinion Analogy

When a pathologist sees an unusual tumor, they consult multiple specialized references and reach a consensus. SingleR with four references does exactly this — using multiple independent "expert opinions" and resolving conflicts with domain knowledge.

Step 05d — TAM Identification

Tumor-Associated Macrophage Discrimination

Custom Algorithm

TAMs were distinguished from circulating monocytes using a Delta_MM score (MacroLin score − MonoLin score), z-score normalized globally. TAM criteria (all three mandatory):

  1. Delta_MM z-score ≥1.0–1.8 (adaptive threshold to ensure ≥30 TAMs)
  2. Expression of ≥3 TAM hallmark genes (C1QA, C1QB, C1QC, APOE, TREM2, SPP1, SIGLEC1, MARCO, MRC1, CD163) above gene-specific medians
  3. Not in the top 10% for DC (FLT3, IRF8, CLEC9A) or neutrophil (CXCR2, MPO) signatures

Result: 211 TAM cells identified. Monaco-annotated DCs explicitly excluded from TAM consideration regardless of other scores.

Step 06

BayesPrism Deconvolution

Step 06 — Deconvolution

BayesPrism — Cell-Type-Specific Expression from Bulk

Three-Phase Process

What it does: Bulk RNA-seq gives an average of all cell types in the tumor mixed together. BayesPrism unmixes this using the scRNA-seq reference to estimate: (1) what fraction of each tumor sample is each cell type, and (2) what gene expression profile each cell type has in each specific patient.

Three separate deconvolution phases were run: Non-Immune (whole-tumor reference), Immune Coarse (coarse immune labels), and Immune Fine (fine immune cell states from CD45+ reference).

⚙️ BayesPrism Settings
Gibbs sampling: 2,000 iterations Burn-in: 500 iterations Thinning factor: 2 Outlier threshold: 0.05, cut: 0.01
💡 Why BayesPrism and Not CIBERSORT or MuSiC?

vs CIBERSORT: CIBERSORT returns only cell proportions, not cell-specific expression profiles. We need to know not just "how many CD8 T cells" but "what are those CD8 T cells expressing" to calculate signature scores per cell type. BayesPrism provides both.

vs MuSiC: BayesPrism's fully Bayesian framework naturally handles uncertainty in both the reference and the bulk data, producing credible intervals alongside point estimates — essential for downstream hierarchical modeling.

🍲 The Recipe Reverse-Engineering Analogy

Consider a bowl of mixed curry, with the question: "What are the proportions of chicken, potatoes, and sauce, and what does each ingredient taste like individually?" CIBERSORT reports only the proportions. BayesPrism reports both the proportions AND estimates the individual flavor profile of each ingredient in that specific bowl.

⚠️ Key Limitation

Deconvolution estimates are not directly measured — they are inferred from bulk data using the scRNA reference. Obesity-specific cell states may not be fully represented in the reference, which was built from patients of mixed BMI status.

💡 Why Build a Custom Database?

Standard gene set databases (MSigDB, ImmPort) are built for general immunology, not for studying obesity-driven dysfunction in pancreatic cancer specifically. No existing database combines cell-type-specific metabolic, stress, and functional signatures tuned to the biology of obesogenic TME remodeling.

Redundancy removal: Pairwise overlap was calculated using the Overlap Coefficient (O = |A∩B| / min(|A|, |B|)). Signature pairs with O > 0.50 were flagged for manual review and consolidation.

Step 08

LLM-Assisted Signature Refinement

Step 08 — LLM Refinement

Google Gemini API for Signature Curation

gemini-2.5-flash

What it does: For each pair of signatures with Overlap Coefficient > 0.50, a Python framework querying the Gemini API (gemini-2.5-flash, temperature = 0.2) assessed whether to retain both, merge, or remove one — given the biological context of "obesity and pancreatic cancer."

Also used for: Proposing new signatures relevant to obesity-driven dysfunction in PDAC. Each proposed signature required 8–12 genes with <20% overlap with any existing signature in that cell type.

💡 Why Use an LLM Here?

Manual curation of 2,143+ signature pairs would require thousands of expert hours. The LLM serves as a first-pass biological reasoner. The low temperature (0.2) ensures reproducible, conservative recommendations. Critically: all LLM suggestions were manually reviewed before acceptance.

👩‍🔬 The Research Assistant Analogy

The role is analogous to a highly knowledgeable research assistant who has read every immunology paper published in the last 20 years. Handed two gene lists and asked, "Given the focus on obesity in pancreatic cancer, are these capturing the same thing or different aspects of it?", the assistant gives a reasoned recommendation — but the human authors, as domain experts, make the final call. That's exactly how the LLM was used here.

⚠️ Responsible AI Use

The Gemini API use here is as a computational tool for curation assistance, not as an intellectual contributor — analogous to using BLAST for sequence alignment. All final scientific judgments were made by the human authors.

Step 09

Signature Score Calculation

Step 09 — Z-scores

Standardized Z-score Approach with Winsorization

Data Processing
⚙️ Score Calculation Pipeline
1. Mean-center gene expression (no log transform) 2. Compute per-gene Z-score for each cell type 3. Winsorize at ±3 (prevent extreme values from dominating) 4. Signature score = mean Z-score across all detected signature genes 5. Missing genes imputed as 0 (neutral contribution) 6. Minimum 4 detectable genes required (else signature excluded)
💡 Why Winsorize at ±3?

Extreme outlier cells would inflate signature scores and potentially dominate the subsequent Bayesian model. Capping at ±3 standard deviations keeps the signal biologically interpretable: a Z-score of ±3 already represents an extreme departure from average, and anything beyond that is likely technical artifact or biologically irrelevant extreme.

🌡️ The Thermometer Analogy

If a faulty sensor occasionally reads -200°C or +500°C, these readings would destroy the average. Winsorization is like saying "anything below -30°C gets recorded as -30°C and anything above +60°C gets recorded as +60°C" — the data stays interpretable and extremes don't distort the analysis.

Step 10

Stabl — Machine Learning Feature Selection

Step 10 — Stabl

Stability-Based Feature Selection (v1.0.0)

Python Package

What it does: Stabl addresses the high-dimensional problem of 2,143 signatures × 140 patients. It runs logistic or LASSO regression 500 times, each time on a different random 80% subsample of patients ("bootstrap"). A signature is "stable" if it gets selected consistently across these 500 runs — not just in one lucky draw.

⚙️ Stabl Settings
Bootstrap iterations: 500 Train/test split: 80% / 20% Preprocessing: >50% missing → excluded; remaining → median imputation; low-variance features → removed Categorical mode: Logistic regression (which sigs discriminate BMI groups?) Continuous mode: LASSO regression (which sigs scale linearly with BMI?) Final selection: Union of categorical + continuous selections
💡 Why Not Just Run LASSO Once?

Single LASSO runs are unstable — small changes in the data can flip which features get selected. With only 140 patients and 2,143 features, a single run is especially unreliable. Stability selection over 500 bootstrap runs identifies features that are consistently associated with BMI across many different "versions" of the dataset.

🗳️ The Election Analogy

Consider running 500 different elections in slightly different constituencies (each one missing 20% of voters). A candidate who wins most of those elections is genuinely popular. Stabl identifies the "genuinely popular" signatures — the ones that consistently associate with BMI regardless of which 80% of patients are sampled.

Step 11

Bayesian Hierarchical Modeling

Step 11 — Bayesian Model

PyMC with NUTS — Quantifying BMI Effects

Python / PyMC

What it does: Two models were built in PyMC: a categorical model comparing groups (Normal, Overweight, Obese) and a continuous model treating BMI as a dose-response variable. Both models use a hierarchical structure where information is shared across related signatures and cell types.

⚙️ MCMC Sampling Settings
Sampler: NUTS (No-U-Turn Sampler) Chains: 4 independent chains Tuning: 2,000 steps (discarded) Sampling: 2,000 steps × 4 chains = 8,000 total samples Target acceptance rate: 0.99 Convergence: R-hat < 1.01, ESS > 400, E-BFMI > 0.3
💡 Why Bayesian and Not Mixed-Effects Models (lmer)?

vs lmer/nlme: Mixed-effects models give a point estimate for each effect with a p-value. Bayesian models give the full posterior distribution of each effect. With n=18 obese patients, this uncertainty quantification is critical. Bayesian also naturally regularizes through priors, preventing overclaiming from small samples.

vs simple ANOVA: ANOVA doesn't handle the nested structure (many signatures per cell type per patient) and doesn't naturally regularize — it would identify many false positives at this scale.

🌡️ The Weather Forecast Analogy

A frequentist mixed model is like saying "tomorrow's temperature will be exactly 28°C." A Bayesian model is like a weather forecast: "There's a 65% chance temperatures will be between 25–31°C, most likely around 28°C." The full probability distribution is far more useful than a single number.

Step 12

TimiGP — Cell-Cell Interactome Analysis

Step 12 — Interactome

TimiGP Framework — Immune Interaction Networks

R Package

What it does: TimiGP infers cell-cell communication networks from bulk RNA-seq data using gene pairs derived from immune cell marker signatures. It runs Cox proportional hazards regression for each gene pair to identify prognostically relevant interactions, then enriches for significant cell-cell interaction modules.

⚙️ TimiGP Analysis Settings
Signatures used: Bindea et al. 2013 (28 cell types), Newman LM22 (22 cell types), Zheng et al. 2021 pan-cancer T cells Normalization: log-transform + geometric mean normalization Cox selection: raw p < 0.01; top 5% of gene pairs by p-value Enrichment: Benjamini-Hochberg FDR < 0.05 Permutation FDR: n = 100 iterations Groups: Normal Weight and Overweight only (n=18 OB insufficient)
💡 Why Cox Regression for Cell-Cell Interactions?

TimiGP's innovation is connecting immune cell interactions to patient outcomes. By testing gene pairs in Cox regression, it identifies interactions where the balance between cell types actually predicts patient survival — going beyond simply counting cell types to identifying the functional interactions that are clinically relevant.

🤝 The Ecosystem Analogy

In an ecosystem, it's not enough to count wolves and rabbits separately. What matters is their interaction. TimiGP similarly asks: does the balance between T cells and macrophages predict whether a patient does well or poorly? This interaction-level analysis reveals dynamics invisible when looking at each cell type in isolation.

Step 13

GigaTIME — Virtual Multiplex Immunofluorescence

Step 13 — Spatial Analysis

GigaTIME — AI-Generated Spatial Tissue Maps

NestedUNet / HuggingFace

What it does: GigaTIME is a multimodal image-to-image translator with a NestedUNet (UNet++) architecture. It was trained on 14,256 patients across 24 cancer types and generates 23-channel virtual multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) images directly from standard H&E histology slides — without requiring actual antibody staining.

Model: Available at huggingface.co/prov-gigatime/GigaTIME. Whole-slide images obtained from the NCI Imaging Data Commons (IDC) using idc-index (V23).

⚙️ Inference Settings
Tile size: 256×256 pixels, stride: 128 pixels (50% overlap) Upscaling: 256→512 (ImageNet normalization) → downscale back 512→256 Aggregation: Logit averaging at overlapping positions Threshold: sigmoid > 0.5 for binary activation Tissue detection: mean pixel density < 220 = tissue positive Section selection: highest CK channel density (proxy for tumor content) DAPI normalization: marker density / DAPI density (correct for variable area)
💡 Why Virtual mIF Instead of Real mIF?

Real multiplex immunofluorescence on FFPE tissue requires specialized equipment, trained technicians, expensive antibody panels, and months of protocol development. The CPTAC-PDAC cohort has H&E slides available, not mIF slides. GigaTIME extracts spatial information from H&E slides that already exist.

🎨 The Colorization Analogy

Old black-and-white photographs contain all the structural information of a scene, but no color. Modern AI can colorize them by learning from millions of paired color/B&W images. GigaTIME does the same: trained on paired H&E/mIF data from 14,256 patients, it has "learned" what protein expression patterns correspond to what tissue morphology features in H&E.

⚠️ Key Limitation

Ground-truth paired mIF data from the CPTAC-PDAC cohort is not available, so pixel-level accuracy cannot be formally validated in this specific cohort. Spatial metrics derived from GigaTIME should be considered as directional signals requiring validation with actual mIF data in future studies.

💡 Why Spearman AND Pearson?

Spearman correlation is robust to outliers and non-normal distributions. Pearson is more powerful when the relationship is truly linear. Running both: if both agree, the relationship is robust; if they disagree, the relationship may be driven by outliers or non-linearity. A metric was considered significant if either test reached p < 0.05.

⚙️ Categorical Comparisons
Continuous BMI: Spearman + Pearson correlation (primary + secondary) BMI groups (3-way): Kruskal-Wallis test Multiple comparisons: Benjamini-Hochberg correction Pixel subsampling: max 5,000 pixels per marker (fixed random seed)
📍 Spatial Distance Computation

Stromal-trapped CD8+ T cells were defined as cells whose centroid falls within Transgelin+ (fibroblast) pixels. Their distance to the tumor boundary was computed using a k-d tree — a data structure that efficiently finds the nearest neighbor in a large point set. Think of it as the GPS shortest-path calculation from each T cell to the nearest tumor boundary pixel, done simultaneously for thousands of T cells.
